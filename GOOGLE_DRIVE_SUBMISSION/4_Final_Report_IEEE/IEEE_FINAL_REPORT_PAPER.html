Automated Food and Produce Freshness Detection System for FIFO Inventory Optimization Using Controlled-Augmentation YOLOv11n
Trần Lê Xuân Mai
Faculty of Information and Communication Technology (ICT), VN-UK Institute for Research and Executive Education (VNUK)
The University of Danang, Da Nang, Vietnam — Student ID: 24020011 | Email: 24020011@vnuk.edu.vn
Abstract—Manual quality inspection of perishable fruits and vegetables in supermarkets and cold-chain warehouses is labor-intensive, subjective, and prone to false-negative sorting errors, impeding strict First-In-First-Out (FIFO) inventory rotation. This paper presents an end-to-end, real-time object detection and freshness grading system built upon the ultra-lightweight YOLOv11n architecture (2.60M parameters, 6.4 GFLOPs). To ensure robust generalization without corrupting critical visual spoilage cues, we introduce a 5-criteria Data Quality Audit featuring cryptographic MD5 anti-leakage verification across dataset splits and a Controlled HSV and Mosaic Augmentation regimen (hsv_h = 0.005, hsv_s = 0.3, hsv_v = 0.3, mosaic = 1.0, close_mosaic = 5). Focusing on three representative produce archetypes across six classes (Fresh/Rotten Apple, Banana, and Bell Pepper), our fine-tuned YOLOv11n model trained via Transfer Learning with the AdamW optimizer and Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule achieves an overall Precision of 98.6%, Recall of 100.0%, mAP@50 of 99.5%, and mAP@50-95 of 96.7% on the independent test partition while operating at 27.4 ms per frame (~36 FPS) on a standard CPU. The trained checkpoint (5.21 MB) is deployed in a modular local execution suite encompassing batch CLI inference and a live webcam web dashboard with real-time HSV and surface dark-spot analytics.
Index Terms—Object Detection, YOLOv11, Food Freshness Grading, Controlled Data Augmentation, Computer Vision, Smart Warehousing, FIFO Inventory Optimization.

I. Introduction

Post-harvest spoilage in retail supermarkets and quick-commerce distribution centers causes substantial economic and environmental waste worldwide. Despite advances in warehouse automation, fresh produce quality control still relies predominantly on manual human inspection. Under continuous conveyor throughput, human grading suffers from visual fatigue and subjective inconsistencies, frequently allowing bruised or moldy items (False Negatives) to remain in storage, accelerating cross-contamination and disrupting First-In-First-Out (FIFO) stock rotation.

Single-stage deep convolutional object detectors provide a promising foundation for automated optical sorting. Nevertheless, applying generic object detection pipelines directly to produce freshness grading introduces four domain-specific pitfalls:

  • 1) Chromatic Sensitivity to Augmentation: Unlike rigid objects that remain invariant under large hue shifts, produce freshness is encoded directly in peel coloration. Default Hue (hsv_h) augmentation shifts fresh red or yellow fruit into brown necrotic tones, corrupting ground-truth labels.
  • 2) Laboratory Bias and Occlusion: Public datasets often contain isolated studio photos of single fruits, degrading accuracy when multiple items touch or overlap on conveyor belts.
  • 3) Annotation Noise and Split Leakage: Community datasets frequently suffer from empty .txt label files and duplicate augmented images leaked across partitions.
  • 4) Edge Hardware Constraints: Warehouse stations require compact weights (<10 MB) capable of real-time CPU or embedded edge inference.

In this paper, we address all four challenges by engineering an audited, end-to-end freshness detection system built on Ultralytics YOLOv11n [1].

II. Dataset Survey and Quality Audit

A. Strategic Six-Class Taxonomy

We base our taxonomy on the Roboflow Freshness Detection repository (freshness-detection-rhrze, >21,000 raw annotated images) [2]. Rather than training on dozens of unbalanced fruit categories, we curate a balanced six-class Proof-of-Concept (PoC) benchmark spanning three distinct visual surface archetypes: Apple (glossy spherical surface), Banana (curved geometry with melanin dark-spot decay), and Bell Pepper (multi-lobed body with green calyx/stem), each across Fresh and Rotten states.

B. Automated Five-Criteria Data Quality Audit

Before training, our automated audit script (step2_quality_audit.py) enforces five strict quality gates: (1) Validity—purges empty .txt files and out-of-bounds coordinates outside $[0, 1]$; (2) Class Symmetry—verifies balanced bounding-box distribution across 174 local benchmark images (120 train, 30 valid, 24 test; 367 boxes) at 50.3% Fresh : 49.7% Rotten (Fig. 1); (3) MD5 Anti-Leakage Check—confirms 0 duplicate MD5 hashes across train, valid, and test; (4) Chromatic Control—locks hsv_h = 0.005 while varying hsv_s = 0.3 and hsv_v = 0.3; and (5) Lab Bias Mitigation—enables 4-image Mosaic compositing (mosaic = 1.0) [3].

III. Methodology and YOLOv11n Architecture

We adopt YOLOv11n [1] (101 fused layers, 2,598,266 parameters, 6.4 GFLOPs). Its backbone integrates C3k2 cross-stage partial blocks, SPPF, and a Cross-Stage Partial with Spatial Attention (C2PSA) module that focuses spatial attention on subtle localized bruises and fungal patches, while a PANet neck fuses multi-scale features into a decoupled detection head.

Initializing from MS COCO pretrained weights (yolo11n.pt) [5], we fine-tune for 20 epochs (imgsz = 256, batch = 16, seed = 0) using AdamW [4] (lr0 = 0.001, lrf = 0.01, Cosine Annealing LR). Mosaic is disabled in the final 5 epochs (close_mosaic = 5) to sharpen bounding-box boundaries.

V. Deployment, Limitations & Future Work

The 5.21 MB checkpoint powers both batch CLI tools (detect.py, test_model.py) and a live Web/Webcam dashboard (web_app.py) rendering color-coded boxes (Green = Fresh, Red = Rotten, Fig. 4) plus HSV and dark-spot percentage analytics.

Fig. 4. Sample YOLOv11n inference output on unseen test produce (Green: Fresh, Red: Rotten).

Physical limitations—2D single-view blindspots on bottom surfaces, internal core rot invisible to RGB sensors, and specular peel glare—will be addressed in future work via 360° roller multi-camera rigs, Near-Infrared (NIR) hyperspectral fusion, and TensorRT deployment on NVIDIA Jetson Nano with 3-tier FIFO ERP routing (Grade A: Store, Grade B: Immediate FIFO Dispatch, Grade C: Reject).

VI. Conclusion

Combining MD5 zero-leakage auditing, color-preserving HSV/Mosaic augmentation, and AdamW Transfer Learning, our YOLOv11n system achieves 99.5% mAP@50 and 100.0% Recall at ~36 FPS on CPU, providing a practical, reproducible AI solution for FIFO produce quality control.
